Diving Into the Real World of AI

February 25, 2026 • 2 min read

The Journey Begins: Why I’m Diving Into the Real World of AI.

A lifelong fascination with machines has led me to finally explore the world of artificial intelligence—not just as a user, but as a creator. This is my roadmap into understanding how these systems work, what they can become, and how I plan to build meaningful things with them.

Since I was a kid, I’ve been fascinated by machines—not just what they do, but why they work. While most people saw technology as something to use, I saw it as something to understand. That curiosity never left. I’ve spent my entire career inside tech companies—some building products, others delivering services like telecommunications that quietly connect millions of people every day.

Yet despite living so close to technology, I’ve never stepped directly into the arena where the real creation happens: building something new. Something that didn’t exist before.

That’s why I’m starting this journey into the world of AI.

For most of history, the idea of a machine that could think—even a little—belonged to science fiction. And then, almost overnight, it became part of everyday life. People now use AI the way they use electricity: automatically, without thinking about what makes it possible.

But I want to understand the part no one sees—the gears behind the magic. How does something we built learn to act in ways that feel almost human? What else could we build if we understood it deeply? And how far can this go?

Most people treat AI as a tool to make their tasks easier. But behind that convenience is a universe of ideas waiting to be explored. I want to dive into that universe—not just to satisfy curiosity, but to eventually build things that help people solve real problems.

This is the beginning of my roadmap. I’ll share what I learn, the detours, the breakthroughs, and probably a few mistakes along the way.

If you’re curious too, stay close. Read the newsletter. Follow the journey. There’s a lot ahead—and it’s going to be interesting.
